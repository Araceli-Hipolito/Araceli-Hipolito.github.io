01

Context & Challenge

Crosswalk was a B2B SaaS / PropTech platform designed as a business intelligence solution for commercial real estate.

Real estate professionals were managing property and market information across spreadsheets and disconnected tools. The goal was to bring that information into a single platform where agencies could monitor, analyze, compare, and manage their data.

The design challenge went beyond centralizing information. Commercial real estate involves large amounts of interconnected data—including properties, units, occupancy, residents, alerts, market information, and portfolio performance.

The experience needed to make this complexity easier to understand and navigate without removing the information professionals depended on.

02

Goal & My Role

The business goal was to design and deliver an MVP that centralized commercial real estate intelligence and gave agencies tools to manage properties, market surveys, portfolios, alerts, and reports.

I worked as the Product Designer on the Agave Lab team, partnering closely with a Product Owner and collaborating with Crosswalk's U.S.-based Business team.

My responsibility was to translate business requirements, domain knowledge, and prioritized product flows into information architecture, UX, interaction patterns, wireframes, prototypes, and high-fidelity product interfaces.

I also collaborated with Engineering and QA throughout the MVP to refine the experience and support implementation.

03

From Concept to MVP

Crosswalk started as an early business concept with an existing brand identity and initial product flows.

Working with the Product Owner and the U.S.-based Business team, I helped translate commercial real estate knowledge, requirements, and early workflows into the structure of the product.

Because the MVP did not include a formal user research phase, domain understanding came primarily from ongoing collaboration with stakeholders who had direct experience working with commercial real estate agencies.

04

Designing for Complexity

One of the most important UX challenges was presenting large amounts of interconnected information without overwhelming users.

Instead of treating every data point independently, I focused on creating hierarchy and organizing information around what users needed to understand, compare, or do next.

Information Hierarchy

Surface decision-relevant metrics first while keeping supporting information accessible.

Scannable Data

Use tables, filters, status indicators, and consistent patterns to make dense information easier to navigate.

Actionable Views

Structure information around user decisions rather than simply exposing raw data.

05

The Product Experience

Home / Dashboard

Created portfolio-level visibility into property performance and activity, including occupancy, available units, current residents, notifications, alerts, filtering, and data export.

Market Survey

Organized building and property information into structured views that supported market comparison, status tracking, creation dates, updates, and ownership.

Create Market Survey

Turned a complex market-comparison workflow into a guided, repeatable process with clear creation states and empty states.

Portfolio

Centralized property management through filtering, property status, locations, unit information, imagery, and detailed property data.

Additional product areas included Reports, Profile, and Programs.

06

Product System & Iteration

As the MVP evolved, I established the initial design system to create consistency across the platform and support additional product areas.

Reusable patterns helped standardize tables, filters, statuses, navigation, forms, and information displays across data-heavy workflows.

Product flows and interfaces evolved through continuous feedback from the Crosswalk team. As business requirements and domain-specific use cases became clearer, I iterated on information hierarchy, interaction patterns, workflows, and UI.

07

Launch, Outcome & Learnings

The team delivered the Crosswalk MVP, transforming an early business concept into a functional B2B SaaS product for centralizing commercial real estate property and market intelligence.

The product created a foundation for managing properties, market surveys, portfolios, alerts, and reporting within a single environment.

Following delivery, Crosswalk moved into fundraising efforts, using the product as part of the company's investment story.

Domain expertise is part of UX

Complex enterprise products require understanding the language, relationships, and operational realities behind the interface.

Complexity needs hierarchy

Data-heavy products cannot always become simpler by removing information. Structure and hierarchy make complexity manageable.

Build systems early

Establishing reusable patterns during the MVP helped maintain consistency as the product continued to evolve.
